### What holds, and one cross-episode pattern

- After the opening, both curves are flat for 3 to 5 minutes (001 about 25%, 002 about 35%): people who stay through the
  context chapters stay through the simulation. YouTube's relative retention figure peaks at 36 to 41% in both videos
  (001 0.40 at 269 to 307 s; 002 0.91 at 243 to 277 s), exactly where "minute by minute" and "day by day" begin. Honest read:
  the simulation chapter is the strongest part of both videos; the work is getting more people there.
- Both episodes lose another 10 to 20 points moving from the finale and aftermath into a final REAL HISTORY chapter at
  about 80%. 003 repeats the layout (c8 "when it stayed unused" at 88%), so it is a free third observation once 003 has data.
- The only text mapped to a paragraph: the 001 window includes the paragraphs beginning "What you are about to watch is a
  simulation" and "Start with what exists today. Russia and the United States hold almost nine in every ten nuclear
  warheads"; the 002 window includes "What you are about to watch is a simulation..." and "Taiwan imports about
  ninety-seven percent of its energy".
- Shorts retention, for reference: carrier 10 kt, 6 minutes, Omaha and 11 days of gas all keep 58% to 88% to the end;
  quarantine falls from 120% to 47% by 21% of its length (about 9 s) and ends at 20%. The Short that opens with a defined
  term and no number or image of a consequence lost most viewers within 9 seconds.

- Wide intervals: 001 is 29 of 1,289 (about 1.5% to 3.2% at 95%); 002 is 13 of 257 (about 2.7% to 8.5%). They only barely

- Confound 1: 001's custom thumbnail never uploaded (metadata.json, 2026-11-04: 403, "channel phone-verified?"), so 001
  ran on an automatic frame. 002 has no upload warning. The 2.3% versus 5.1% gap compares a default frame with a designed
  one. That is the most probable explanation and 001's thumbnail may still be unset.
- Confound 2: 001 got 1,249 impressions on its first day and almost none after (97% drop); 002 got about 250. YouTube
  sized the test pool differently and I cannot see why.
- Thumbnail variants: there is no per-variant data. `pipeline.py` calls `pkg(ep, thumb="a")` so only variant a (001
  "27 MINUTES TO OMAHA" globe, 002 "FIRST NUKE SINCE 1945" map) was ever uploaded; b and c have never been shown.
  Variant testing is currently impossible, so "which variant works" has no answer.
- Titles: 001 "Russia Launches a Nuclear Strike on America... What Happens Next?" is a template The Nuclear Clock already

## 5. Recommendations (ranked by expected impact)

Process stays as is. Every item below is a proposed test with a pass mark, because the samples (21 to 35 viewers per
curve, 6 Shorts) are too small for rules. 003 is already rendered; apply tests to 004 onward and compare against
the 001 to 003 curves.

1. Test a shorter, moving opening on 004. Evidence: both long videos lose the audience in the first 80 s, 54 of 97
   points in 001 (7 to 82 s) and 47 of 88 in 002 (40 to 74 s), on the same sequence of 11 to 14 s static disclaimer card,
   title globe, chapter card, statistics. Test: keep the spoken "simulation" and "it has not happened" (required by
   CLAUDE.md) but put them over live map motion with a small corner tag instead of a 10 s text card, shorten the
   sentence, and place the first number that carries stakes (a flight time or a count) before the first chapter card.
   Also give chapter 1 a story opening rather than a total. Measure: audience watch ratio at the 0.11 bin against 0.43
   (001) and 0.41 (002); 0.55 or better with at least 100 views is a real change. Do not read anything from fewer than
   about 50 views.
2. Re-upload the 001 thumbnail, and from 004 record which thumbnail variant ships. Evidence: 001's
   `upload_warnings` shows the custom thumbnail 403 on 2026-11-04, so the 2.3% CTR is a default-frame result; 002 uploaded
   cleanly. The CI path hard-codes variant a, so b and c have no CTR. Test: after the 001 thumbnail is set, watch its daily
   CTR in `data/reach/`; for 004 to 006 rotate variants a, b, c by episode and write the variant into `metadata.json`;
   swapping a thumbnail mid-life on one video is a better test but needs about 1,000 impressions per arm to see a 2 point
   difference. This needs a code change outside data/ that the showrunner or growth owner has to make.
3. Test the Shorts mix: make all three Shorts simulation-consequence clips with a number in the first second, and swap
   the slot order once. Evidence: the four Shorts with a simulated blast, countdown or number have 82% to 122% average
   viewed and 97 to 1,199 views; the two explainer clips (4 times launched by mistake, quarantine) have 41% and 40%
   average viewed and 37 and 80 views. Both explainers were the 19:00 slot (s3) so slot and content are confounded; put the
   best blast clip in the 19:00 slot on 004 and the explainer clip at 09:00 and compare. Win: the 19:00 slot reaches at
   least 40% of the best slot's views.
4. Build the Shorts-to-long bridge and measure it. Evidence: RELATED_VIDEO is 5% of views and 26% of watch minutes,
   about 3.3 minutes per view. Test: log RELATED_VIDEO and YT_OTHER_PAGE views and minutes per day, and try one variant of
   the Short description that puts the long-video link in the first line (the CLAUDE.md rule only says each description
   links the long video). A pinned comment with the link on the best Short (hK24hiHogvs) costs nothing. Win: RELATED_VIDEO
   views per Short view up from 50 per 930 (5.4%).
5. Test an earlier start of the simulation on 005 or 006. Evidence: both curves are flat after the opening and relative
   retention peaks when the simulation begins (4:16 on 002, 4:46 on 001). Compress chapters 1 to 3 to under 3 minutes
   and compare average percentage viewed (24% and 36% now) and average view duration (3:00 and 4:02) with a 100 or more view
   sample. Keep the order real data, then simulation.
6. Test the position of the closing REAL HISTORY chapter. Evidence: both episodes lose 10 to 20 points at about 80% on the
   switch into a history chapter, and 003 has the same shape. Test on 004: shorten that chapter to 60 s or put it before
   the simulation and end on the clock. Needs 003 data first (use it as a third check before changing anything).
7. Avoid the "X Launches a Nuclear Strike on Y... What Happens Next?" template unless the premise is new. Evidence from
   competitors only: it produced 245K and 91K for The Nuclear Clock on fresh premises and 978 and near zero when
   re-skinned. Keep 003's number-plus-named-city pattern and compare CTR on 003 with 002.
8. Measurement fix: store the full 100-point retention curve (1% bins, about 7 s) and per-day analytics in snapshots. At 5%
   bins the first-80-seconds loss cannot be placed to a paragraph, which is the main question here. No effect on viewers.
