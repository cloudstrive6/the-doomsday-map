## C. Simulation assumptions (SIMULATION tag, stated as assumptions in the script)

Decided by the scenario designer. Everything below is a simulation choice unless it cites an A or B row. No calendar date appears anywhere: the script says "hour zero", "hour six", "day four" (A3, A4 collision risk). The scenario is not a prediction, it has not happened, and there is no public evidence that any hostile actor has switched off Taiwan's grid (A1).

**Engine.** `scenario.json` has zero strikes: no nuclear, no EMP, no missile. `t0_utc` is 13:00 UTC (21:00 in Taipei, full dark, so the engine sun puts Taiwan on the night side; `scenario.computed.json` sun lat 21.62, lon -13.62). The date in the file is a placeholder for lighting only and is never shown or spoken. The engine gives no blast, burn or fallout numbers for this episode; none may be typed by hand. The only numbers the script may use are the sourced A and B rows and the labelled simulation choices below. Day-after fallout wind: not applicable, there is no fallout.

**Why a cyber episode with no strike.** Lane 3 (infrastructure and cyber). A nuclear or EMP element would turn it into 002 and break the lane logic. The escalation here is the point: a grid attack is deniable, reversible and below the nuclear threshold, so the simulation asks what a country can do to another without a single missile.

### Escalation logic (why this sequence)

1. Pre-positioning exists first. Taiwan's NSB counts about 2.63 million intrusion attempts a day, energy rising fastest (A5, A6); the US advisory on Volt Typhoon shows the method of waiting inside operational technology for a crisis (A8). The simulation assumes access was already in place, as an assumption, not a finding.
2. Evening peak, summer, thin reserve: about 7 percent night margin (A15), summer demand far above winter (A16). A small loss matters more.
3. Attack the grid, then attack the repair, as in Ukraine 2015 (B1), with direct breaker control as in 2016 (B2) and a comms hit one hour before the real event as in Viasat 2022 (B3).
4. Fabs lose to glitches as well as outages (A26, B5, B8). Chips are the world-scale consequence, grid is the island-scale one.
5. No gas cut: a grid-only attack does not stop LNG arriving, so the 11-day gas clock (A11) is NOT started by this scenario. It is the reserve that keeps restoration possible, and the episode must say so (see "the thumbnail number" below).

### Hour-by-hour (all SIMULATION; sizes are choices)

- Hour 0 (21:00 local, a summer night). Malicious commands open breakers at a set of southern substations and generation trips follow. Assumed loss: about 10 GW of supply, chosen to match the one real anchor, the 10.5 GW lost in 2022 (B6). Say "about as big as the 2022 fault", never "the attack takes 10 GW" as a fact. Hero visual: the island map going dark city by city, Kaohsiung first, then north (no rings; engine has none).
- Hour 0 to 1. Fabs: the voltage dip hits Hsinchu, Taichung and Tainan science parks before backup systems stabilise. In-process wafers are lost; any count is the simulation's, so the script gives none (A26 anchors only that dips matter). Taipower and the grid operator lose remote control; operators switch by hand, as in 2015 (B1).
- Hour 1 to 3. Second wave: wipers and corrupted firmware on control systems and on call-centre lines, as in 2015 (B1), and an attack on satellite backup terminals as in Viasat (B3). Two to three undersea cable segments are cut by ships, modelled on Matsu 2023 and the 2025 case (B7, B9). Taiwan reroutes over what remains (A21, fifteen cables, SINGLE-SOURCE, so say "more than a dozen" at most or skip). Number of cuts is a simulation choice, intent is assumed, and real cases are disputed (A22, A23).
- Hour 6. Restoration begins with island-wide manual switching; about half the lost supply is assumed back in the north first, rotating blackouts in the south. Compare: the 2022 outage was largely restored the same night (B6; check the row before quoting a duration), Ukraine 2015 took 1 to 6 hours (B1).
- Hour 12 to 24. Hospitals and water pumping on diesel (oil stock about 140 days, A12, so fuel exists but delivery needs working roads and phones). Fabs: tools intact, networks corrupted, so restart is days not hours. The simulation sets the corrupted-network case closer to 2018 (B5) than to the quick 2024 quake restart (B8, over 70 percent of tools back in about 10 hours with intact tools).
- Day 2 to 3. Rotating cuts continue; banks and card payments degrade (B4 shows card payments can fail). Exports of leading-edge chips are interrupted. The script may say "the world's supply of the most advanced chips" (A18: 62 to 92 percent, depending on who counts), not a share-of-world number of our own.
- Day 4 to 7. Partial restoration, fab output reduced rather than zero. Whether the world "runs out" of anything is not stated: no published model covers this.

### The hero (no nuclear): one fab district

The close-up chapter is not "one warhead, ring by ring". It is "one voltage dip, step by step" on one science park (Hsinchu, region or city shot; check `video/public/city/` before choosing a city slug, none was found for Taiwan on 2026-11-20, so use a region shot or fetch one with `node scripts/fetch-city.mjs`). Steps, each tied to a row: (1) a dip of a fraction of a second is enough to spoil wafers in progress (A26); (2) backup systems take over if they are working (A26, April 2021); (3) corrupted networks, not dead power, set the restart time (B5); (4) intact tools restart in about a day (B8). Engine numbers: none; do not invent.

### The thumbnail number

"Eleven days" is the gas buffer (A11), not electricity (D). In this scenario it is the time the island has to restore the grid before a fuel clock would matter if shipping were also hit. Frame it as: "the grid attack does not touch the gas, but it takes away the island's ability to move it; Taiwan has about eleven days of gas". If the script cannot tie the clock to a mechanism, it should use another number (about 10.5 gigawatts in 2022, B6). The mechanism offered: LNG terminals and pipelines run on power and controls, so a long grid outage could stall regasification (SIMULATION assumption, not sourced; flag it).

### The day after (aftermath on the map, from 2026-11-09 rule)

- What stays dark: rolling cuts in the south for days; hospitals and water pumping on diesel; traffic signals and rail off in blocks. Frame as "what a grid operator would have to choose", not as a casualty claim.
- What stops working: card and phone payments, remote control, cloud and cross-strait data, once cables and backup satellite terminals are hit (B3, B7, B9, B4).
- What the fabs cannot make: leading-edge chips for phones, cars and servers (A18, A19, with A19 labelled a blockade study, floor over 2 trillion dollars, never applied to this scenario).
- The response: repair ships and cable crews (B7: weeks to months for Matsu), emergency generation, allies sharing grid forensics. The world's response is unknown; say so.
- Closing line must land on the dark map and the reserve clock, not on a history or statistics card.

### What the script must NOT claim

- No death toll, no injury count, no GDP loss for this scenario (A19, A20 are blockade and war studies; none was found for a blackout).
- No "China did it" without "in this simulation"; no named living victims.
- No wafer count or per-day chip loss (D).
- No statement that the attack could succeed; only that these methods have worked elsewhere at smaller scale (B1, B2, B3) and that real Taiwan grid failures have happened without attack (B6).
- Not a prediction and not timed to any real event (A3, A4).
