# Episode 004 research: One 800-kiloton airburst over one US city, ring by ring

Compiled 2026-11-09. Every claim the script uses is listed with its sources. Two reputable, independent sources per fact is the rule; single-source items are marked SINGLE-SOURCE and must be softened in the script ("about", "one estimate puts it at") or cut. Where sources disagree, the range is given with who says what. Source status is stated honestly: URLs marked "(opened)" were fetched and read this session; URLs marked "(search listing only, not opened)" appeared in a search result but could not be read, so the fact rests on the snippet and must be treated as softer. Simulation assumptions are in section C and must be labelled SIMULATION on screen. Blast, burn and fallout numbers come only from the scenario engine, never from this file.

**Framing check, read first.** As of 2026-11-09 nothing real has happened that changes the framing: no nuclear weapon has been used in war since 1945 and no explosive nuclear test has been reported in the last 60 days. What HAS changed, and the episode can use honestly: (1) New START, the last treaty capping US and Russian strategic arsenals, expired on 2026-02-05, so for the first time since 1972 there is no legally binding cap (A4); Russia says it keeps to the old limits only while the US does (A4). (2) The UN First Committee ended its 2026 session on 2026-11-03; the US was the only state to vote against a resolution urging the nuclear test ban treaty to enter into force, and 12 states including the US, UK and France voted against a resolution on the humanitarian consequences of nuclear weapons (A23). (3) Testing talk: in October 2025 Trump ordered the Pentagon to start testing "on an equal basis"; Energy Secretary Chris Wright then described the work as non-nuclear; the US has kept a voluntary moratorium on explosive testing since 1992 and no explosive test has occurred (A25, A26). (4) Russia says a Sarmat ICBM regiment goes on duty by the end of 2026 (A8). (5) The Bulletin set the Doomsday Clock at 85 seconds on 2026-01-27, the closest ever (A21). The episode must NOT say a nuclear test or strike is under way. Upcoming real dates the script and metadata must not collide with: Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists annual gathering 2026-11-12 (search listing only, not opened); CTBTO on-site inspection field exercise in Namibia to 2026-11-20 (A26); the Hiroshima and Nagasaki anniversaries 2027-08-06 and 2027-08-09; the Feb 2027 anniversary of New START's expiry; the January 2027 Doomsday Clock announcement. ## C. Simulation assumptions (SIMULATION tag)

Everything here is a simulation choice, not a fact and not a prediction. Every blast, burn and fallout number comes from `scenario.computed.json` (engine: Glasstone and Dolan scaling calibrated to NUKEMAP Hiroshima figures, A15). It has not happened.

### Escalation logic

There is none, on purpose. This episode is the single-warhead case: one weapon, one city, no war plan. The scenario asks only what one warhead does, ring by ring. The attacker is deliberately unattributed ("an unattributed submarine", location simulated, Atlantic) so the episode names no country as aggressor and stays out of forecasting. It is the smallest case on the scale that A19 (Princeton Plan A) and A20 (Nature Food) describe at the far end; the script may say so and cite them, not us.

Order of arrival for the ring walk: the flash and fireball first, the thermal pulse within seconds, the blast wave after, travelling as sound does, so the 1 psi ring hears it last. Do not quote arrival seconds; the engine gives none. Say "seconds" and "tens of seconds" and no more. Hiroshima comparison from A11: a firestorm formed over about 4.4 square miles at 15 kt, so the script may say a firestorm is possible here but must not claim one is certain or give its size.

Fallout: an air burst produces little local fallout (engine: "little local fallout", A16). The wind is a simulation assumption: from the west-northwest (about 290 degrees), blowing toward the east-southeast, so any light fallout drifts over Lake Michigan and toward southwest Michigan and northwest Indiana. Prevailing winds at this latitude are generally westerly, but that is not a weather forecast. The engine gives no plume dimensions for an air burst, so the plume shot, if used, is labelled "illustrative", and the script says a ground burst would be a different, far worse fallout story (A16). Official advice applies regardless of burst type: get inside, stay inside, stay tuned, at least 24 hours (A17).

### The day after (what the episode ends on)

Only what A17, A18 and published guidance support, stated as what planners expect, never as a count:
- Hours one to twenty-four: the response is shelter and information. FEMA planning guidance focuses on the first 24 to 72 hours (A17). Stay inside; radiation levels fall rapidly in the first day (A17).
- Hospitals: the US has about 2,000 specialist burn beds in the whole country (A18, "about"). No study here models the surge, so the script says the gap, not a number of patients.
- What stops working inside the rings: the script may say power, phones and roads in the heavily damaged zone would be expected to fail, framed as what the damage rings imply (buildings down, lines down), not as a measured outcome. No outage figure, no grid statistic, no EMP claim (a 800 kt air burst at this height is not an EMP scenario).
- The response: help arrives from outside the zone, and evacuation is ordered by officials; people should not return until told (A17). Name no agency head and no living person.
- Day two and day three: the city is a stretch of rubble in the middle and intact outskirts, with the rest of the country untouched by this one weapon. Close on the aftermath, not on arsenal statistics, the Clock or history.

### What the script must not claim

- A death toll, injury count or population-in-ring figure for Chicago (none from a published study for this city and yield; A15 NUKEMAP caveat).
- That 800 kt is a US warhead, or that any named country fired it (A6, D).
- That the strike is likely, imminent, or tied to a real date.
- A burst height in metres, an arrival time in seconds, a firestorm size, or a fallout dose or footprint for the air burst (the engine supplies none).
- That one warhead causes climate effects or famine (A20 is thousands of warheads).
- Any statement about the real defences, alert status or response of Chicago agencies beyond the public guidance in A17.

## D. Figures the script must NOT use

- "The US has an 800-kiloton warhead" or any named US warhead at 800 kt (false; the US range is about 8 kt to 1.2 Mt, A6; 800 kt is only the top of the Russian SS-18 range, A7).
- "Hiroshima was a 20-kiloton bomb" (20 kt is the Hiroshima Committee's nominal figure; its own estimate is about 13 kt; OSTI says 15 kt; 21 kt is Nagasaki, A9, B2). Use "about 15 kilotons".
- "Hiroshima's burst height was X metres" (580 m from OSTI versus 280 m from the Hiroshima Committee; do not state it, A9).
- "Hiroshima killed 80,000 instantly" or any single exact toll (use "about 70,000 at once, well over 100,000 by the end of 1945", A10).
- "Tsar Bomba was 100 megatons" (100 Mt was the design; the test was 50 Mt, A14).
- "Castle Bravo was predicted at X megatons" (sources say 5 or 6 Mt; say "far more than predicted", A13).
- "The Goldsboro bombs were 24 megatons" (up to 3.8 Mt each, B4) or "one switch alone prevented it" stated as settled fact (B4).
- "Arkhipov alone saved the world" as fact (contested; National Security Archive and later accounts say the launch order was not passed on; B6). Say "helped prevent".
- "Petrov saw hundreds of missiles" (the system showed one, then four more; five in total, B7).
- "Plan A predicts 91 million deaths" (34.1 million dead and 57.4 million injured, first hours only, A19).
- "A nuclear war would kill 5 billion with blast" (the Nature Food figure is starvation after soot from thousands of warheads, not one, A20).
- "The world has 13,000 warheads" (about 12,200 in 2026, A1).
- "New START is still in force" or "Russia and the US are bound by treaty limits" (expired 2026-02-05, A4).
- "The US has resumed nuclear testing" or "Russia has tested a nuclear warhead in 2026" (no explosive test reported; the US moratorium since 1992 stands, A25, A26).
- "The Sarmat is deployed" (not on combat duty as of March 2026, A8).
- "The Doomsday Clock is at 90 seconds" (85 seconds since 2026-01-27, A21).
- Any casualty, injury or fatality count for the simulated strike (none from a published study for this city and yield; engine numbers describe ring areas only).
- Any real upcoming date for the simulated strike (the Nov 2026 to Feb 2027 dates in the framing check).
