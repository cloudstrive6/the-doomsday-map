**Framing check, read first.** Searched 2026-11-26 for the last 60 days (US and China, Taiwan, Iran war, munitions, production). Nothing real changes the hypothetical framing, but this episode's premise is partly real, so read this carefully.

1. No US-China shooting has happened. No missile has been fired at US forces by China, and no US strike on China has occurred (A1). Reuters, via IBTimes, reported that Taiwan-side assessments "do not establish" that China has decided on a blockade or military action (carried from 015 A1).
2. The munitions shortage is real, published and recent. The US-Israeli war on Iran (Operation Epic Fury) began on 2026-02-28 and the US used a large share of its long-range missiles and interceptors; CSIS published the inventory estimates on 2026-04-21 (headline) and 2026-04-24 (paper) (A2 to A9). The war is not over: an April ceasefire and a June memorandum collapsed and US strikes resumed in July; as of 2026-11-12 Al Jazeera describes "no war, no peace", with the US naval blockade of Iranian ports still in force and Qatari mediation under way (A2). The script must not say the Iran war is over or that a deal is done. The honest line is that the Pacific stocks were drawn down while a second war was on.
3. China: from about 2026-11-02 the PLA ran a large unannounced naval deployment in the Western Pacific (more than 100 navy and coast guard vessels counted by Taiwan on 2026-11-03, about 70 by 2026-11-13); no named exercise has been announced (carried from 015 A2; IBTimes search result and Taipei Times 2026-11-04 search result confirm the November activity). Taiwan's intelligence chief has said the PLA routinely drills in November and December.
4. Diplomacy: Trump and Xi met in Shenzhen on 2026-11-19 at APEC (their third meeting in 2026), and a first Trump-Xi-Putin trilateral followed the same day. No arms-control deal came out of it; the White House statement said a nuclear war "cannot be won and must never be fought". Per Xinhua, Trump said the US "does not support Taiwan independence" (A36). New START expired in February 2026.
5. Upcoming real dates the episode must not collide with: Taiwan's local elections on 2026-11-28; the PLA's usual November to December drill season; the unresolved Iran talks (Qatar mediation); the Pentagon's fiscal 2027 budget and munitions contracts still moving through Congress. The Pentagon says China wants to be able to "fight and win a war on Taiwan by the end of 2027" (carried from 002 A27); the brief forbids using 2027 or any real date for the simulated crisis, so the simulation runs on day numbers only. Re-check the news on the day of upload, especially the Iran status.

## D. Figures the script must NOT use

- "The US would run out of missiles in a week" as a flat claim. The 2023 CSIS game says the US ran out of some anti-ship missiles (LRASM) in 3 to 7 days and JASSM-ER at about 30 days (A18). The "under a week" phrase in "Empty Bins" refers to some long-range precision weapons, and the 5,000 missiles in three weeks is Jones's summary of the games (A19).
- "The Iran war used up half the US stockpile." CSIS says four of seven key munitions may have lost more than half (CSIS does not name the four; Patriot, THAAD and PrSM ranges reach or pass half, A8 to A10), but JASSM is about a quarter, SM-6 16 to 32 percent and Tomahawk about a third (A5 to A10). Do not say "half" for the missile stock overall.
- "The US has run out of Tomahawks" or "has no JASSMs left". CSIS's estimate is about 2,100 Tomahawks and about 3,300 JASSMs remaining before new deliveries (our subtraction of CSIS's own numbers), and the "all in the region" language refers only to what was in theatre (A5, A6).
- "The Iran war is over." It is paused or unresolved: a blockade and shaky talks continue as of 2026-11-12 (A2).
- "US Tomahawk production is 1,000 a year." That is a target (A12). Current output is 79 a year on CSIS's five-year average, under 200 in AP's account, about 60 in another report (A11).
- "THAAD or Patriot interceptors are exhausted" as fact. CNN and Reuters-based estimates give about 80 percent and "about half" depletion; CSIS gives ranges (A8, A9).
- "China has 5,000 missiles" or any single Chinese stockpile or production number stated as fact. The Pentagon's last count is at least 3,150 ballistic and about 300 ground-launched cruise missiles for 2024; the 2025 report did not update it; production is not published (A21, A25).
- "China is ahead of the US by 10 to 1 in missiles" or similar ratios (not sourced).
- "The DF-26 is a nuclear missile" (it is dual-capable; most are conventional; A22, A27). "The DF-17 is nuclear" (FAS says dual-capable claims are unsubstantiated; carried from 002).
- "China has 1,500 warheads" or "600 deployed warheads" (see 002 D: about 620 total, about 34 deployed per SIPRI via 002 A28).
- "The US has 355 ships" (291 in May 2026, A15) and "7th Fleet" mistakes already listed in 002 D.
- "War in 2027" or any date for the simulated crisis (brief and CLAUDE.md forbid it). The Pentagon's "by the end of 2027" is a capability goal, not a plan or a forecast (A34).
- "Trump said the US opposes Taiwan independence." Beijing's readout says "does not support", and Washington had not published its own readout (A36).
- "Japan has Tomahawks" (the order of 400 is delayed; a destroyer was modified; A5, A31).
- "CSIS predicts the US would lose a Pacific war." CSIS wargames are games with stated assumptions (see 002 D).
