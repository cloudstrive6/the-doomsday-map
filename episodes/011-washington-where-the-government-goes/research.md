# Episode 011 research: A simulated nuclear strike on Washington, and where the government goes in the first 30 minutes

Compiled 2026-11-18. Every claim the script uses is listed with its sources. Two independent reputable sources per fact is the rule; single-source items are marked SINGLE-SOURCE and must be softened in the script ("about", "one estimate puts it at") or cut. Where sources disagree, the range is given with who says what. Only URLs that were actually opened are listed; where a fetch failed or a site blocked the fetch, the fact is marked SINGLE-SOURCE or left out. Simulation assumptions are listed separately and must be labelled SIMULATION on screen. "Our haversine" means a great-circle distance we computed from published coordinates (Raven Rock, Mount Weather and Camp David coordinates from Wikipedia infoboxes, White House 38.8977 N 77.0365 W).

**Framing check, read first.** As of 2026-11-18 nothing has been fired at, tested near or threatened against Washington, and no continuity-of-government activation has been reported in the last 60 days. The simulation is not a prediction. What HAS happened, and the script must not collide with it:

1. **Real nuclear diplomacy is live this week.** A first-ever Trump, Xi and Putin three-way meeting on nuclear arms is planned on the sidelines of the APEC summit in Shenzhen, which runs 2026-11-18 to 2026-11-19. Al Jazeera (2026-11-14) says the White House confirmed it and Trump wants a three-way deal on stockpiles; a senior US official told Reuters "We do not expect to get to any kind of agreement." Search coverage puts it on Thursday 2026-11-19 (SINGLE-SOURCE for the exact day; Al Jazeera says only "next week"). The episode must not be published as if it were reacting to, or predicting, that meeting. Re-check the outcome on the day of upload (A1, A2).
2. **No treaty limits.** New START expired in February 2026 (A3). The US and Russia are in the first year with no binding limits on strategic forces.
3. **Russia is signalling, mostly at Europe.** On 2026-09-29 and 2026-09-30 Russian embassies and a formal note to NATO threatened nuclear use if NATO tried to cut off Kaliningrad; on 2026-10-06 Lithuania passed the first of three votes to repeal its constitutional ban on hosting nuclear weapons, with the final vote expected in January 2027 (A4). Russia tested the Sarmat ICBM on 2026-05-12 (A5). None of this is a strike or a test explosion.
4. **Testing talk.** Trump said on 2025-10-30 he had told the Pentagon to start testing nuclear weapons "on an equal basis"; I found no 2026 report of an explosive test by any state. Do not say any state has resumed nuclear explosive testing (D).
5. **Also in the news, not our subject:** an ongoing US war with Iran is on the APEC agenda (Al Jazeera, 2026-11-14). The script should not mention it as cause or context unless the scenario designer decides to; the brief does not use it.

Dates the episode must not sit on: 2026-11-18 and 2026-11-19 (APEC and the trilateral), the January 2027 Lithuanian vote, and any year-end "Sarmat enters service" claim (Putin says by end of 2026). The simulation uses no calendar date (see C). The next-scenario line on the end slate is Europe, which sits close to the Kaliningrad threats; keep that line generic with no date.

## D. Figures the script must NOT use

- NUKEMAP press figures for Washington, such as "120,000 deaths for 15 kt downtown", "177,650 killed by 100 kt on Capitol Hill" or "461,240 killed by 1 Mt on the Pentagon". They are tool outputs in secondary press, not published studies, and the tool's author says they are "evocative, not definitive" (A26). The engine's rings only.
- "Plan A kills 34 million in 5 hours" and "91.5 million casualties in 3 hours" as exact claims: sources differ on timing (headlines say five hours, other write-ups three hours of casualties). The safe line is "more than 90 million dead and injured in the first few hours", citing Princeton (A22). Plan A does not model Washington.
- "The President has 3 minutes" or "a 6-minute decision window": the figures come from unreliable pages; use "less than ten minutes", labelled an analyst estimate (A8).
- "Submarine missiles reach Washington in 5 minutes": the published range is about 10 to 15 minutes (A7). The "under five minutes" figure is a Russian state TV claim about a hypersonic weapon (A15).
- "Raven Rock is inside Camp David" or "Raven Rock is 6 miles from Washington": it is about 6 miles from Camp David and about 61 miles from the White House (A13). One page (Wikipedia) even contradicts itself on the location.
- "Mount Weather is 64 miles from Washington" or "51 miles" as a single precise figure: use "about fifty to sixty miles" (A14). Mount Weather's Area B "600,000 square feet" is single-source (Grey Dynamics).
- "The Greenbrier bunker holds 1,100 people": the 1,100 in Wikipedia's article refers to hotel televisions, not the bunker (A23). No source in this file gives the bunker's capacity.
- "Congress used the Greenbrier" or "the government still uses it": it was built 1959 to 1962, exposed by the Washington Post in 1992 and decommissioned at once. "Never used" is supported by one NPR page I could not open, so say "was never needed".
- "9/11 was the only time Mount Weather was activated": Wikipedia and the FAS say 1965 was the only full-scale activation before 2001, and 2001 involved leaders being flown there; do not claim a count.
- "The Washington Post named the shadow government's bunkers": it did not; it agreed to a White House request not to name the two principal sites (A20).
- "The 75 to 150 officials" as a hard number: SINGLE-SOURCE; say "dozens to about a hundred and fifty".
- "The E-4B is being replaced next year" or any in-service date for the E-4C: not in service; the Air Force date was not confirmed in an opened source (A12).
- "The US has 3,700 deployed warheads" or "Russia has 6,000": FAS 2026 says 1,670 deployed strategic for the US and 1,796 for Russia, with stockpiles of 3,700 and 4,400 and total inventories of 5,042 and 5,420; the stockpile is not the deployed figure (A6). The ACA, the SIPRI and the FAS differ by edition; always say "about".
- "New START is still in force" or "was cancelled by Trump": it expired in February 2026 (A3). Trump has said he wants a new deal.
- "Russia or the US has resumed nuclear explosive testing": no report of an explosive test by either; Trump's 2025-10-30 post was ambiguous (A27).
- "The Trump, Xi and Putin meeting produced an arms deal": it had not taken place as of 2026-11-18; officials said they expected no agreement (A1). Re-check on the day of upload.
- "Washington has a missile shield": no source found (A25); do not state it either way.
- "Congress can appoint replacements for dead House members": the House can fill seats only by special election; governors can fill Senate seats (A18). Proposed amendments have not passed to our knowledge, but that is not sourced.
