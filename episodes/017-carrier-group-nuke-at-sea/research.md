# Episode 017 research: One 10-kiloton nuke at sea, and a US carrier strike group

Compiled 2026-11-24. Reworked from episode 007 (dropped by the visual gate, not by the audience). Every row was carried over, re-checked where the page would open, and upgraded to a second source where one could be found; the rest keep their SINGLE-SOURCE mark and must be softened ("about", "one estimate") or cut. Many US government pages (navy.mil, history.navy.mil, USNI News) returned 403 or certificate errors on 2026-11-24 and could not be opened. Where sources disagree the range and who says what is given. No blast, burn or fallout radius is given on purpose: those numbers come only from `python -m studio scenario`. The Crossroads, Wigwam and Swordfish figures are history, not engine output.

**Framing check, read first.** Searched the news on 2026-11-24 for the last 60 days (carriers, naval nuclear weapons, arms control, nuclear testing).

1. Nothing real changes the framing. No nuclear detonation, nuclear test or nuclear incident at sea has happened. The last US explosive test was 1992 (A21). The October 2025 order to resume "testing" was clarified by the Energy Secretary on 2025-11-02 as system tests with noncritical explosions, not nuclear explosions. The script must not imply a bomb was tested.
2. Real carriers are busy and politically sensitive. USNI News headlines (search results 2026-11-24, pages returned 403): carrier USS George Washington left the Middle East on 2026-11-18 after three months; USS Theodore Roosevelt is now in the US Central Command area and was filmed alongside George Washington on 2026-11-17; USS George H.W. Bush has been in the Arabian Sea since April. TWZ (opened earlier, 2026-10-17) reports a US campaign against Iran, "Operation Epic Fury", from 2026-02-28 (SINGLE-SOURCE). Lesson: never name a real hull, deployment, or operation as the target; use a generic "US carrier strike group", a fictional unnamed attacker, and put the burst far from any real operating area.
3. Arms-control calendar. New START expired in February 2026 with no successor (A31). A Moscow Times report of 2026-11-11 (citing SCMP; opened for 007) said Russia, China and the US were preparing a trilateral talk on AI in nuclear command and a "nuclear war cannot be won" declaration at the APEC meeting in Shenzhen, reported as 2026-11-18 to 2026-11-19; that date has passed and no outcome was found. Do not use "this week" language, no calendar date in the script, and set the simulation at an unspecified time.
4. Naval-nuclear news that is real but must not be mistaken for fact about our scenario: the US Navy nuclear sea-launched cruise missile (SLCM-N) is in development (A13); Russia claimed tests of Burevestnik and Poseidon in October 2025 (A20). Neither is a fielded anti-carrier nuclear weapon of record. Upcoming dates to avoid: none known for 2026-11-25 to 2026-12-31 beyond ordinary carrier movements.

## C. Simulation assumptions (SIMULATION tag, stated as assumptions in the script)

Decided 2026-11-24 by the scenario designer, reworked from 007 (same single strike, simpler picture). Every blast, burn and fallout number below is copied from `scenario.computed.json` (studio/scenario.py, Glasstone and Dolan scaling calibrated to NUKEMAP). Nothing here is a forecast; no calendar date appears on screen.

**Escalation logic.** Deliberately minimal: the episode is about one weapon, not a war. An unnamed, fictional attacker (no country, no flag) fires one nuclear anti-ship cruise missile from a submarine at a generic US carrier strike group in open ocean. Why:
- Cold War doctrine put nuclear weapons aboard ships and aimed them at carrier groups (A8 to A11); the US took its own off surface ships by 1992 (A7, B7), so the group has no nuclear weapon to answer with from its decks.
- The nearest real brushes were at sea: B-59 with a 10 kt torpedo (B5) and the Belknap fire (B6). 10 kt matches the Swordfish nuclear ASROC (A30, B4, single-source yield) and B-59.
- No attacker, country, hull or operation is named (D11, D12). The weapon is "a fictional submarine-launched anti-ship cruise missile": no published source gives a yield for any modern anti-ship weapon (D3).
- The simulation stops on the aftermath at sea and the decision on the bridge, not on retaliation. It does not choose a response.

**Strike (one row, `sea-burst`).**
- t0 2027-02-10 14:30 UTC, about 00:30 local at the burst point: night, so the flash lights the sea. Engine sun point 14.78 S, 33.86 W. Not a real event date; the script says "minute zero" and never states the date.
- Launch point (simulated, 24.0 N, 152.2 E) and burst point (simulated, 24.0 N, 155.0 E) are open Pacific. Engine: distance 284.4 km, bearing 89.4 degrees, cruise class, flight 20.1 minutes, impact at minute 20.1. The sub is "location simulated".
- Yield 10 kt, air burst (inputs). Baker (A24) and Wigwam (A28) were underwater and Able (A23) was an air burst at about 23 kt, so this is a different case from Baker; the script must say Baker was shallow, underwater and 23 kt. The engine does not model burst height; its rings assume the height that maximises the 5 psi ring.

**Formation (inputs, not sourced; one picture for the director).** A hexagon of ships, drawn to scale from the engine rings:
- Carrier at the centre of the formation (the 0 km mark). Generic group (A4): one carrier, six escorts (a cruiser plus destroyers, drawn all alike), one supply ship. Real groups vary; the script says "a typical group has three to five escorts" only if it also says this drawing uses six for symmetry, or draws five and leaves one slot empty.
- Six escorts on a circle of 2.5 km radius around the carrier, 60 degrees apart, so each neighbour is 2.5 km from the next. Supply ship 1 km from the carrier on the side away from the burst.
- The burst is over the escort on the east side, the one nearest the attacker (burst bearing from carrier: due east, 2.5 km). The carrier is therefore 2.5 km from the burst, the same distance as the two neighbouring escorts. Compare Able, which missed its aim point by about 650 to 700 m (A23).
- Placement against the engine rings (geometry from the inputs above): burst escort at 0 km (inside the 0.153 km fireball ring); the two neighbours and the carrier at 2.5 km (outside the 5 psi ring 1.459 km and the burn ring 1.617 km, inside the 1 psi ring 3.949 km); the supply ship at 3.5 km (inside 1 psi); the two far-side escorts at 4.33 km and the opposite one at 5.0 km (outside 1 psi). Nothing but the burst escort is inside 5 psi.
- Ship icons are fixed size and exaggerated (a destroyer at true scale is a dot); label this on screen.

**Hero strike: one warhead, ring by ring, at sea (region shot, about 12 to 16 km wide, night sun, `burst`).** Engine numbers for 10 kt air burst:

| Ring | Engine value |
|---|---|
| Fireball radius | 0.153 km (about 300 m across) |
| 20 psi (heavy blast) | 0.297 km |
| 5 psi (moderate blast) | 1.459 km |

Reading it: the escort under the burst is inside the fireball. The carrier and two neighbours sit between the burn ring (1.617 km) and the 1 psi ring (3.949 km): the model says light blast, not heavy blast or third-degree burns on exposed skin. The ring colours are land-damage categories. No open source says how a hardened modern warship responds (A5 gives only the wash-down system); the script must say so and must not say the carrier survives or sinks. Timing (engine flight 20.1 min, the group sees the missile only if radar does) is a choice.

**Engine limits for ships at sea (director and script must respect).**
1. No sea model: no ship hardness, wave or base-surge model, no ship damage states. Rings are radii on a flat map.
2. `burst` takes only air, ground or conventional. Ground means a land surface burst with heavy fallout; do not use it for sea. Air is the only honest choice.
3. Baker's water column, base surge and 94 ft wave (A24 to A26) are history only, not applied to this air burst.
4. Fallout: the engine says "little local fallout" and gives no plume, wind or dose. Any plume direction on screen is a simulation choice and, if drawn, drifts downwind (east, an input) as a faint labelled band; better not drawn. Wigwam (A29, single-source) is history only.
5. Ship positions are director inputs from the formation above.
6. `sunkAt` is a simulation choice, not an engine output. Do not sink the carrier. At most show the burst escort "destroyed" (it is inside the fireball ring) and label it an assumption of this simulation.
7. No city JSON for open sea, so the close-up is a region shot at sea. Avoid sparse frames: keep the group large (ring formation fills the frame) and add a faint grid, scale bar and the legend.

**The day after (SIMULATION, assumptions; nothing is a published outcome).**
- First minutes: flash, then the pressure wave arriving at ships at different times (sound-speed lag is physics, not engine output; say "seconds later" only). General quarters; aircraft aloft are the main surviving strike force. Choices.
- Radiation at sea: the model says little local fallout. History anchors: Able, most ships reboardable within a day (A23); Wigwam downwind ships within dose limits (A29, single-source, soften). "History suggests an air burst's radioactivity is a smaller problem than the blast and fire" as one estimate only.
- Wash-down (A5) is the real feature the crew would turn on (the script says the carrier has one, not that it works here).
- The group disperses and a rescue and medical effort follows (doctrine choices). Reactors: "shut down and checked" as an assumption; no source gives the response.
- What stops working (assumptions): radio and radar near the burst (EMP is not modelled and not claimed); the air wing's ability to land on a damaged deck. Anything more is cut.
- Last scene: the remaining ships, the decision on the bridge, and a world not yet knowing who fired. No death toll, no named ship or person, no response chosen.

**What the script must not claim.** Death tolls or casualty numbers (D11); that the carrier sank or survived; that any real navy or country has such a weapon (D3, A20, D4); that the US Navy has no nuclear weapons at sea (D5, say "no tactical nuclear weapons on surface ships since 1992"); that Baker's effects apply to an air burst; any prediction; any hull number, operation name or real date.
