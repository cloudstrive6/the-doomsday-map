# Episode 021 research: London, one warhead, four submarines, and what the "72 hours" guidance assumes

Compiled 2026-11-28. Every claim the script uses is listed with its sources. Two reputable, independent sources per fact is the rule; single-source items are marked SINGLE-SOURCE and must be softened in the script ("about", "one estimate puts it at") or cut. Where sources disagree, the range is given with who says what. URLs marked "(opened)" were fetched in full or in part for this file; URLs marked "(search result only)" appeared in a search result and were not opened, so treat the fact as needing a second look before it is stated flatly. Simulation assumptions belong to the scenario designer (section C) and must be labelled SIMULATION on screen.

**Framing check, read first.** As of 2026-11-28 there has been NO nuclear strike, test, or attack warning involving the United Kingdom, and nothing in the last 60 days changes the premise that a strike on London "has not happened". Four real developments the script can honestly use, and one that it must not get wrong:

1. On 2026-10-22 the Cabinet Office launched "Be Ready", the first stage of a three-year household preparedness campaign (A2). It asks households to be able to cope for three days if power, water, phone and internet fail. **It is NOT a nuclear campaign.** The gov.uk release names severe weather, storms and wildfires and does not mention nuclear weapons; Al Jazeera's 2026-11-14 analysis says nuclear is not mentioned and the security concerns cited are cyber attacks and hostile-state activity against infrastructure; Defence Secretary Wes Streeting said there was no "specific thing" the government was preparing for (A3, A4). The brief's angle, "what the 72-hour guidance assumes", therefore has to be told as: the guidance assumes ordinary disruption, not a nuclear detonation, and we then show what one warhead does against that assumption. The script must never say the government told Britons to prepare for nuclear war. Also, gov.uk says "three days", not "72 hours"; "72 hours" is the press shorthand (D1).
2. Streeting wrote in the Telegraph (late October 2026, sources give 2026-10-24 to 2026-10-26) that Britain has about 1,000 days to be ready to fight, because adversaries' capacity could allow an attack by 2030, naming Russia (A5). This is a planning deadline, not a prediction.
3. Around 2026-10-28 the Times and Sky News reported three of the four Vanguard-class submarines at sea at once, the first time in over five years; the Ministry of Defence declined to explain it (A16). On 2026-11-15 a deterrent patrol returned home (search result only).
4. The government's 2026 Defence Nuclear Enterprise update (published 2026-09-15) and the 8.4 billion pound Dreadnought Delivery Phase 4 award (July 2026) are the current official picture of the programme (A20, A21).
5. Background that frames the risk: New START expired in February 2026 and no treaty now limits US and Russian arsenals (A27).

Upcoming real dates the episode must not collide with: the Be Ready campaign runs for three years from 2026-10-22, so the episode lands inside it; no scheduled UK nuclear test, SSBN milestone or government civil defence announcement was found for December 2026. Do not give the simulated strike a calendar date, a day of the week, or a season that reads like a real one. The Dreadnought lead boat is not due before the early 2030s, so the simulation must use the Vanguard class. Re-check the news on the day of upload, especially anything linking Be Ready to nuclear risk, any Russia-NATO incident around Kaliningrad (the Arms Control Association reported Russian condemnation of alleged NATO plans for Kaliningrad on 2026-11-01, search result only), and any MoD statement on the submarine overlap.

## C. Simulation assumptions (SIMULATION tag, stated as assumptions in the script)

Decided 2026-11-28 by the scenario designer. Every blast, burn and flight number below comes from `scenario.computed.json` (studio/scenario.py, Glasstone and Dolan scaling calibrated to NUKEMAP Hiroshima figures); nothing is typed by hand. Everything on this list is a simulation choice, not a fact and not a prediction. It has not happened.

**Escalation logic.** The scenario is deliberately small. It starts from the real late-2026 backdrop (Streeting's planning deadline of about 1,000 days, A5; no arms-control treaty since New START expired, A27; the UK deterrent assigned to NATO, A17) and asks one question: what does the household guidance, which is built for three days without power, water and phone (A2, A3), assume about the first hour of a nuclear strike? The script says plainly that Be Ready is not a nuclear campaign (A3, D). No war story is told in detail: a Russia-NATO crisis in Europe is assumed to have run for weeks, Russia is assumed to have decided on a limited strike on the UK's capital and on the base of its deterrent, and the script gives no cause or date. The calendar date on screen is never shown; the clock reads only "minute zero" and hours.

**Strike groups (2 warheads in total, engine totals: 0.2 Mt, first impact 7.6 minutes, last 8.8 minutes).** One shared launch site, a Russian Borei-class submarine in the Norwegian Sea at 68.0 N, 3.0 E (location simulated). Weapon assumed: R-30 Bulava, 100 to 150 kt published estimates (episode 001 research A3, Barents Observer, Moscow Times; a second independent source for yield is thin, so the script says "published estimates run from 100 to 150 kilotons" and that 100 is our choice). The sub-launched missile is chosen because its short flight leaves the least warning, which is the point of the episode.

- Strike 1, `nor-faslane`: one warhead, 100 kt, ground burst on HM Naval Base Clyde, Faslane (56.0675 N, 4.82 W), the home of the four Vanguard boats and, 3 km west, the Coulport warhead depot (A11, A18). Engine: 1,386 km from the launch point, flight 7.6 minutes, fireball 0.48 km, 20 psi 0.51 km, 5 psi 2.51 km, 1 psi 6.81 km, third-degree burns 4.16 km, heavy local fallout (a ground burst). Faslane is hit first because the attack is assumed to try to remove the deterrent. The script does not claim the attack succeeds: one Vanguard is always on patrol at sea (A10, A14), so the boats in port are not the whole force. Whether that boat could or would answer is not simulated.
- Strike 2, `nor-london`, the hero: one warhead, 100 kt, airburst over Westminster and Whitehall (51.5034 N, 0.1276 W). Engine: 1,842 km, flight 8.8 minutes.

**Yield choice, stated as an assumption.** 100 kt is the low end of the published Bulava range (episode 001 A3). It is also the rating given for the US W76 (CSIS), the warhead from which the UK Holbrook is derived (A13), so the viewer can read it as "a warhead of the Trident class". The UK has never published Holbrook's yield, so the script must never say "a Trident warhead is 100 kilotons" or "this is what our own warhead would do" (D). Say: "we assume 100 kilotons, about seven times Hiroshima's" (A25, our arithmetic). A 100 kt airburst is a sub-megaton weapon; larger Russian warheads exist and would give larger rings, which the script may state only qualitatively.

**Hero target: central London, one warhead, ring by ring (engine numbers).** Airburst, 100 kt, 51.5034 N, 0.1276 W (Whitehall). City close-up: `video/public/city/london.json`, 60 km wide, fetched 2026-11-28.
- Fireball: radius 0.38 km (about 770 m across), everything inside vaporised.
- Heavy blast, 20 psi: 0.64 km.
- 5 psi: 3.14 km, an area of 31.0 square kilometres, where most houses collapse. For scale, 31 square kilometres is about 2 percent of Greater London's 1,572 square kilometres (A23, our arithmetic).
- Third-degree burns on exposed skin: 4.16 km.
- 1 psi: 8.51 km, windows shatter and glass injures people far from the blast.
- Fallout: airburst, little local fallout. The script must not warn of heavy fallout over London. It may say that the dust and smoke of a burning city is not modelled here.
The rings are drawn on the map and read out by radius; no count of people inside them is stated, because no published study supplies one for this warhead (D). The script may say how many people live per square kilometre (about 5,800, A23) but must not multiply it by the ring area: that is a hand-made death toll in disguise. Morning rush (08:40) affects where people are, not the engine numbers.

**Warning and timing.** Launch to impact 8.8 minutes for London and 7.6 for Faslane (engine). The script can say "less than nine minutes" for London. It must not say the UK has a four-minute warning (ended 1992, B3) and must not claim what warning systems would or would not reach people in that window, beyond what A3, A6 and B3 support: the gov.uk radiation guidance is "go in, stay in, tune in" and does not mention a nuclear detonation (A6).

**The day after (what the script may say; each line tied to a row).**
- Weather and fallout: the engine has no wind model. We choose a westerly wind, the commonest in southern Britain, as a simulation assumption (not a source): the plume from the ground burst at Faslane would drift east over central Scotland. The script says "if the wind is from the west" and gives no dose, no distance and no list of towns, because the engine produces no plume. London itself is an airburst and sees little local fallout (engine).
- The guidance meets the strike: Be Ready asks for water, food, a torch, a radio and a power bank for about three days (A2); gov.uk radiation advice says stay in for one to two days (A6); 1980 Protect and Survive asked for 14 days in a fallout room, the inner refuge for the first 48 hours (B5). The honest line: the guidance was written for storms and outages, and a nuclear strike is a different category. The script may say what each item would and would not help with, qualitatively, without invented figures.
- What stops working (qualitative only, each limited to what the rings and A-rows support): inside the 5 psi ring most buildings collapse, so services in that area stop; outside it, glass and burn injuries stretch beyond 8 km (1 psi); a morning-rush city loses its centre of government and its transport hub. Electromagnetic effects from an airburst of this size on electronics are NOT simulated and must not be asserted. National government and the Prime Minister's authority over the deterrent (A17) are continuity questions the script may pose but not answer.
- The response: official advice is unknown; the script says "the guidance would say stay in, tune in" at most (A6). No numbers on hospitals, beds or emergency staff are used (none in A or B); if the script wants them it must request research rows first.
- The deterrent: the Vanguard on patrol (A10, A14) continues; the three in the late October 2026 report (A16) is SINGLE-SOURCE and unusual and is not assumed. The script may close on the open question of what the patrol would be ordered to do, with doctrine in A17 ("extreme circumstances of self-defence", deliberate ambiguity). Nothing is decided.
- Ending: the episode closes on the simulation's aftermath (the map of rings over London, then the first hour and the first day under the three-day guidance), not on a history or statistics chapter.

**What the script must NOT claim.** No death toll or injured count for London or Faslane or Scotland; no named living person as a victim; no claim that Russia has planned or named London as a target (D); no claim about Holbrook's yield, range of warhead options or what the UK would do in reply; no claim about whether Trident missiles at sea would survive or fire; no claim the guidance was written for a nuclear attack; no real or upcoming date for the strike; no dose, plume length or town list for fallout; no EMP effects. The cause of the crisis and the decision to strike are simulation inventions and must be labelled so.

**Sun and lighting.** t0 internal clock only, never stated, 08:40 UTC (London local time, GMT), in daylight at a low winter sun; the subsolar point from the engine is 22.97 S, 48.32 E. Night nuclear shots do not apply; the flash is a morning flash, and the script can say the strike falls in the morning rush hour.

## D. Figures the script must NOT use

- "The government told Britons to prepare for nuclear war" or "the 72-hour guidance is a nuclear warning" (false: Be Ready names severe weather and everyday emergencies and does not mention nuclear weapons, A2, A3; Streeting said there was no "specific thing", A4). Say the guidance is about "three days" of disruption and the episode asks what it would mean in a nuclear strike.
- "72 hours" as the official wording (gov.uk says "three days"; "72 hours" is press shorthand; the only official "72 hours" in the sources is the 1980 plan to broadcast the Protect and Survive films if an attack seemed likely within 72 hours, B5, itself SINGLE-SOURCE).
- "The UK has 260 nuclear warheads" (260 is the ceiling set in 2021; the estimate is about 225, A7, A8).
- "The UK has 120 warheads at sea" or "four submarines on patrol" (about 40 warheads are on the one boat at sea at a time; 120 is the operationally available total; one boat is normally in overhaul, A10, A11). The late October 2026 report of three at sea is unconfirmed and unusual (A16).
- "The UK has a no-first-use policy" (false; deliberate ambiguity, "extreme circumstances of self-defence", A17).
- "A Trident warhead is 100 kilotons" as a UK fact (the UK has never published it; the US W76 is 100 kt and the W76-1 about 90 kt; the UK warhead is "believed to be similar", A13).
- "The UK can launch within minutes" (the government says its missiles are not on alert and need several days to prepare, A14; reporting is SINGLE-SOURCE, soften).
- "Trident has a range of 12,000 km" without qualification (12,000 km is CSIS; the US Navy's public figure is about 7,400 km; SIPRI says over 11,000 km in tests, A12).
- "The UK owns its Trident missiles" (it has title to 58 from a pool shared with the US Navy, A12).
- "The Dreadnoughts are in service" or "the new Astraea warhead is deployed" (not before the early 2030s, A20, A21).
- "London would lose X million people in one strike" (no published study in this file gives a figure for a single warhead on London; the 29 million and 8.5 million figures are whole-country Cold War scenarios, A31, B6; GLAWARS figures were not verified, A26).
- "Exercise Square Leg killed or would kill X" as fact (it was an exercise with disputed numbers; sources give 131 or 150 weapons and 205 or 280.5 megatons, B6).
- "Protect and Survive was sent to every home" (about 2,000 copies went to local authorities and police in 1976; a public edition went on sale in May 1980; nothing was delivered to every household, B5).
- "The four-minute warning still exists" (it ended in 1992 and the siren network was largely dismantled, B3; the UK has no nuclear attack siren system today in the sources opened, so say only what B3 supports).
- "Russia has named London as a target" in 2026 (found only in an IBTimes summary, not verified; cut it).
- "The UK is preparing for war with Russia by 2030" (Streeting called it a planning deadline, A5).
- "Greater London has 10 million people" or other round-up figures (about 9.1 million, ONS mid-2024, A23).
- Any date for the simulated strike, any claim that the strike is a forecast, any real named person as a victim.
