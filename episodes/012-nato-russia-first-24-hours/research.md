# Episode 012 research: NATO and Russia, the first 24 hours of a conventional war in Europe

Compiled 2026-11-19. Every claim the script uses is listed with its sources. Two reputable, independent sources per fact is the rule; single-source items are marked SINGLE-SOURCE and must be softened in the script ("about", "one estimate puts it at") or cut. Source note: URLs below were opened in this session unless marked "(search listing, not opened)" or "(403, not opened)"; a fact whose second source is only a search listing is thinly sourced and flagged. Where sources disagree, the range is given with who says what. This file deliberately does not repeat the whole of episode 009's research (`episodes/009-russia-attacks-baltic-72-hours/research.md`: Zapad-2025 detail, Narva, Princeton Plan A split, Danish intelligence timeline, the 2022 Kaliningrad rail ban, Able Archer details beyond B1 here). Where a 009 row is reused it is pointed to by its 009 number. Simulation assumptions are listed separately and must be labelled SIMULATION on screen.

**Framing check, read first.** As of 2026-11-19 there has been NO Russian attack on a NATO member, no Article 5 invocation and no nuclear use in Europe (A1). I found no reporting dated after 2026-11-11 that changes this. What HAS happened in the last 60 days, usable as "why this matters now" with the war itself labelled SIMULATION: (1) on 2026-09-29 to 2026-09-30 Russian embassies in London and Dublin, and a diplomatic note to NATO states (Reuters), said Russia would be ready to use its "entire arsenal, including nuclear weapons" if NATO tried to cut off Kaliningrad, claiming "information that NATO is preparing an air and naval blockade" of it; NATO's Mark Rutte said "stop the nuclear threats" and "Putin knows that he can never win" (A2); (2) NATO's annual Steadfast Noon nuclear exercise ran 2026-10-13 to 2026-10-23 in Italy with 17 nations and no live weapons, announced as routine and unpostponed despite the Russian warning (A3); (3) Lithuania passed the first of three votes to repeal its constitutional ban on hosting nuclear weapons on 2026-10-06, final vote expected January 2027 (A4); (4) on 2026-11-11 Lithuania, Latvia and Estonia briefly issued drone shelter alerts and NATO jets scrambled while Ukrainian drones hit Ust-Luga and Russian-Ukrainian officials talked in Abu Dhabi (A5); (5) search summaries (not opened) report Russian Deputy Foreign Minister Grushko saying on 2026-11-05 that NATO Baltic drills include Kaliningrad blockade scenarios, and a Euromaidan Press headline of 2026-09-17 says Belarus held tank drills near the Suwalki Gap. These two are SINGLE-SOURCE listings: use only as colour, or cut. None of it turns the hypothetical into news, but "Russia has publicly called Kaliningrad a nuclear line" is true and is the real analogue of the simulation's trigger. Do NOT write that Russia "is about to attack": Danish intelligence says a full invasion is highly unlikely near term (009 A23) and NATO's Barrows said NATO has seen no "big changes" in Russia's nuclear posture (A3). Upcoming real dates the script must not collide with (the simulation gives hour numbers only, no calendar date): the final Lithuanian vote on the nuclear ban in January 2027 (A4); any outcome of the Abu Dhabi Russia-Ukraine talks (unscheduled); New START limits have lapsed so any US-Russia arms talks are unscheduled (A21). No Zapad is held in 2026 (the last was 2025-09-12 to 2025-09-16, 009 A7). Re-check the news on the day of upload.

## C. Simulation assumptions (SIMULATION tag, stated as assumptions in the script)

**Escalation logic and why.**
- The spark is real and Russian-framed: Moscow has publicly called a cut-off of Kaliningrad a nuclear line (A2, A19 conditions 3 and 4); NATO denies planning it. In this simulation Moscow acts on its own warning and strikes first, at the Polish and Baltic bases a NATO air campaign against the oblast would need (choice). - Hour 0 to 4, Russia opens (choice): jamming and cyber first (pattern of A5, A27, B5, B6, B7), then conventional missiles: Iskander-M on Malbork Air Base and Adazi Base, a ship-launched Kalibr on Lielvarde Air Base, a conventional Oreshnik from Belarus on Rzeszow-Jasionka, the aid hub (A9, A13). Article 4 at once, Article 5 by about Hour 6 (A6, where each ally decides its own action).
- Hour 5 to 6.5, NATO answers on the Kaliningrad belt (choice): three air-launched cruise missiles on Chkalovsk air base, Baltiysk naval base and the air-defence belt.
