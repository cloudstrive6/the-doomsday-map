# Episode 009 research: Russia attacks the Baltic, the first 72 hours, and the nuclear threshold

Compiled 2026-11-15. Every claim the script uses is listed with its sources. Two reputable, independent sources per fact is the rule; single-source items are marked SINGLE-SOURCE and must be softened in the script ("about", "one estimate puts it at") or cut. Source note: URLs below were opened in this session unless marked "(search listing, not opened)"; a fact whose second source is only a search listing is treated as thinly sourced and flagged. Where sources disagree, the range is given with who says what. Simulation assumptions are listed separately and must be labelled SIMULATION on screen.

**Framing check, read first.** As of 2026-11-15 there has been NO Russian attack on a NATO member, no Article 5 invocation and no use of nuclear weapons in Europe (A1). What HAS happened in the last 60 days, and is worth using as "why this matters now" with the attack itself labelled SIMULATION: (1) on 2026-09-29 and 2026-09-30 Russian embassies in London and Dublin, and a diplomatic note to NATO states reported by Reuters, said Russia would be "ready to use all its arsenal, including nuclear weapons" if NATO tried to cut off Kaliningrad; NATO's Mark Rutte answered "stop the nuclear threats" (A2); (2) on 2026-11-11 Lithuania, Latvia and Estonia briefly issued drone shelter alerts and NATO jets were activated while Ukrainian drones hit Ust-Luga and Russian jamming pushed drones off course (A3); (3) Lithuania passed one of three parliamentary votes to repeal its constitutional ban on hosting nuclear weapons, final vote expected January 2027 (A4); (4) Russian and Ukrainian officials held two days of talks in Abu Dhabi around 2026-11-10 to 2026-11-11 with a new US push to end the war (A3); (5) the US announced on 2026-05-01 a cut of up to 5,000 troops from Germany (A21). Nothing here changes the framing from hypothetical to real, but the Kaliningrad threats are the closest real-world analogue to the simulation's trigger and the episode can honestly say "Russia has publicly said this is a nuclear line". Do NOT write that Russia "is about to attack" or that an attack is planned: the Danish intelligence service says a full invasion is highly unlikely in the near term (A23). Upcoming real dates the script must not collide with (no calendar date is given in the simulation, day and hour numbers only): the final Lithuanian vote on the nuclear-weapons ban in January 2027 (A4); any outcome of the Russia-Ukraine talks (unscheduled); NATO's annual Steadfast Noon nuclear exercise is usually in October (not verified this session, do not cite). Zapad, the Russia-Belarus exercise, was last held 2025-09-12 to 2025-09-16 and is normally every four years, with no 2026 Zapad found (A7). Re-check the news on the day of upload.

## C. Simulation assumptions (SIMULATION tag, stated as assumptions in the script)

Decided 2026-11-15 by the scenario designer. Every number below comes from `scenario.computed.json` (studio/scenario.py) or a cited row. Everything not cited is a simulation choice and the script says "in this simulation". Scenario clock: Hour 0 is the first deniable move; t0 (the first nuclear launch) is Hour 61.

**Calendar and light.** `t0_utc` 2027-03-09T01:40Z (03:40 in Lithuania), a dark pre-dawn hour; the engine sun is far from the Baltic, so night shots are correct. No date is shown on screen, only "Hour N", so it cannot read as a prediction or collide with the January 2027 Lithuanian vote (A4) or the Abu Dhabi talks (A3). Hour 0 is therefore about 2027-03-06 in the file only.

**Escalation logic and why.**
- Hours 0 to 2, deniable first move (choice, in the Crimea 2014 pattern, B1): unmarked troops and a "protect Russian speakers" pretext at a border town (Narva, A28, used only as geography), cyber and jamming, Kaliningrad crisis framed as Moscow's claim that NATO is cutting it off (A2, A31). Russia denies involvement at first, as in B1. Precedents for the grey-zone opening: drones and MiG-31 incursions (A5, A6, B8).
- Hours 3 to 11, conventional missile strikes (choice): Iskander-M (A13, about 350 km, dual-capable) and a sea-launched cruise missile hit Rukla and Siauliai in Lithuania, Amari and Tapa in Estonia, to blind air policing and pin the battlegroups (A20, A32). Targets are real public base locations; the script gives no damage or casualty claims.
- Hours 12 to 48, the push (choice): Russian and Belarusian forces close the Suwalki Gap (A9) from both sides; the single-track Polish-Lithuanian rail line (A9) and the thin forward force (A20) slow reinforcement; the German-Netherlands corps (A22) and a smaller US contribution (A21) are still arriving. Article 4 early and Article 5 by about Hour 12 (A8, the 2025 Article 4 cases A5, A6). Hour 31: a conventional Iskander strike on the Orzysz training area in Poland, the Gallant Boar 2026 site (A29), on the Polish approach to the Gap.
- Hour 48 to 60: RAND's games found Russian forces at the outskirts of Tallinn or Riga in at most 60 hours under the 2016 posture (A24). The script may say "RAND's 2016 games" and may use the 60 hours as the longest they saw. It must NOT say today's NATO would lose in 60 hours (A20, A22, D).
- Hour 61, the threshold: in this simulation Moscow judges the war is turning against it (NATO air power and the German-Netherlands corps arriving) and fires one low-yield Iskander-M with a nuclear warhead at Siauliai Air Base, the NATO air-power node, to "stop the fight" with no city hit. That is one analysts' reading of "escalate to de-escalate" (A18, contested, never stated as policy) and of doctrine condition 4 (A17). The 2022 "special combat duty" order (B6), the Able Archer and Norway rocket cases (B2, B4) are the real history of how readily either side reads a launch as nuclear.
- The simulation stops at the decision, as 002 does. The three options shown are real categories only: a nuclear reply in kind, a conventional reply, a pause and talks. The script does not pick one.

## D. Figures the script must NOT use

- "Russia has attacked / will attack the Baltic" or any real date for an attack (false; Danish intelligence says a full invasion is highly unlikely, A23). The 2026-11-11 drone alerts were stray drones in a Ukraine-Russia exchange, not an attack on NATO (A3).
- "Russia would take Riga in 60 hours" as a prediction (it is RAND's 2016 game outcome, the longest time seen, under the 2016 posture, A24). Also do not say "36 hours" as a RAND finding: it is Foreign Policy's phrasing of the range.
- "Russia has 6,000 nuclear weapons" (the 2026 military stockpile is about 4,400; the 5,000-plus figure includes about 1,020 retired warheads awaiting dismantlement, total about 5,420, A14). "Russia has 1,550 deployed warheads" is the treaty limit; the FAS estimate is about 1,796 deployed (A14, A19).
- "Russia has 2,000 tactical nuclear weapons" as a fact (1,000 to 2,000 range, FAS about 1,794 and an earlier CRS quote of 1,477, A15).
- "Russia has stationed nuclear warheads in Belarus" as an established fact (Russia and Lukashenko say so; the Bulletin finds no conclusive public evidence, A16).
- "Escalate to de-escalate is official Russian doctrine" (disputed; the published doctrine does not use the phrase, A18).
- "The Iskander can reach Berlin" (about 350 km range, Kaliningrad to Berlin about 530 km, A10, A13). Some export-version figures say 500 km; Berlin still lies outside even that from Kaliningrad.
- "The Suwalki Gap is 100 km wide" or other popular figures: the usual figure is about 65 km (A9).
- "Zapad 2025 had 200,000 troops" (that was 2021; Putin claimed about 100,000 for 2025, other estimates are far lower, A7).
- "NATO has 40,000 troops in the Baltics" (no such confirmed figure; about 10,000 in one 2026 report, A20).
- "Estonia invoked Article 5" (it requested Article 4 consultations in 2025, A5; Article 5 has not been used against Russia).
- "Princeton predicts 91 million dead in a Baltic war" (Plan A is a US-Russia strategic exchange, deaths plus injuries, a model not a forecast, A26).
- "NATO is preparing to blockade Kaliningrad" (Russia's claim, denied by NATO, A2). Say "Russia says".
- Any Eisenhower-style or Reagan-style direct quotation from B2 beyond the short phrase "genuine anxiety" (attributed to McFarlane in one source only).
