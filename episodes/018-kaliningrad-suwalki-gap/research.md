**Framing check, read first.** As of 2026-11-25 there has been NO NATO move against Kaliningrad, NO Russian attack on a NATO member, no Article 5 invocation and no nuclear use in Europe (A1). What HAS happened in the last 60 days, usable as "why this matters now" with the cut-off itself labelled SIMULATION: (1) on 2026-09-29 and 2026-09-30 Russian embassies in London and Dublin said Russia would be ready to use "all its arsenal, including nuclear weapons" if NATO tried to isolate Kaliningrad, and Reuters reported the same warning in a formal note to NATO states; NATO's Mark Rutte answered "stop the nuclear threats" and NATO's top commander said on 2026-10-12 that NATO has "no need and incentive to escalate" (A2); (2) over the weekend of about 2026-11-21 to 2026-11-22 Kaliningrad sounded air-raid sirens and restricted Khrabrovo airport after a "suspicious object" was said to approach from the Lithuanian side; the governor's own inspection found no threat, and Lithuania's defence minister called the online claims of an attack an attempted provocation (A3, SINGLE-SOURCE for the dates); (3) on 2026-11-15 Estonia announced it will close all three border crossings with Russia from 2026-12-01 after an alleged GRU-ordered arson attempt on a drone maker (A4); (4) on 2026-10-06 Lithuania passed the first of three votes to repeal its constitutional ban on hosting nuclear weapons, the final vote expected in January 2027 (A5). None of this turns the hypothetical into news, but "Russia has publicly called a cut-off of Kaliningrad a nuclear line" is true and is the honest hook. Do NOT write that NATO plans to blockade Kaliningrad (NATO denies it; the claim is Russia's) and do NOT write that Russia is about to attack. Upcoming real dates the simulation must not collide with: 2026-12-01 (Estonian border closure takes effect), January 2027 (final Lithuanian nuclear-hosting vote, no day set) and the fifth anniversary of the Ukraine invasion on 2027-02-24. The simulation should use "Day N" or "Hour N" only, with no calendar date on screen. Re-check the news on the day of upload: this story is moving week to week.

## C. Simulation assumptions (SIMULATION tag, stated as assumptions in the script)

Decided 2026-11-25 by the scenario designer, from the research desk's suggestions. Every number below comes from `scenario.computed.json` (studio/scenario.py). NATO denies planning any move against Kaliningrad (A2); the cut-off, every date, strike and yield below is the simulation's own choice, not a prediction. t0 in the engine is 2027-03-12 16:10 UTC, set only for the dusk light over the Baltic; no calendar date is shown on screen and the crisis runs on "Day N" (clear of 2026-12-01, January 2027 and 2027-02-24, A4, A5).

**Escalation logic and why.** A slow squeeze, then a sudden step, which is the opposite tempo of 009 (hour 61 Iskander on Siauliai) and 012 (hour 22 open-water shot). The pressure is the enclave's lack of any land, grid or free sea link (A9, A22, B8), and the trigger is the one Russia itself has named, isolation of Kaliningrad (A2), argued under doctrine condition 4, a conventional threat to territorial integrity (A18; analysts differ on whether an isolation qualifies, so the script says "Moscow argues").

- Day 1: simulated NATO states halt all rail and road transit to Kaliningrad through Lithuania, modelled on the 2022 transit ban and its 40 to 50 percent of goods (B7), but total this time. The Baltic states have no grid link to the enclave since 2025-02-08 (B8). It reverses the Berlin blockade pattern (B2).
- Days 2 to 7: a maritime inspection line off Baltiysk; Russia answers with ferries from St Petersburg (A22), jamming and cable harassment (A13). The ferry throughput and stock days are not sourced and the script gives no number for them. Moscow sends the note it already sent in real life (A2). The simulation treats this stage as pressure, not yet war.
- Day 8 (strike 1, engine: 122 km, about 8.6 minutes flight): a conventional air-launched cruise missile from a simulated Polish strike package hits Baltiysk, the base on the inspection line. This is the cross of the shooting line. Weapon class from the public JASSM family; no source row, so the script calls it "a cruise missile" and says it is assumed.
- Day 11 (strike 2, engine: 100 km, about 3.0 minutes): Russia answers with a conventional Iskander-M (A11, B5) from a simulated site in Kaliningrad Oblast on the Marijampole rail and road junction on the Via Baltica (A7, A26 names the town), the corridor's transport node. No death toll.
- Day 12: German brigade and allied battlegroup in Lithuania (A20) move toward the corridor; Belarus mobilises at Grodno about 75 km from Suwalki (A17). The 65 km gap (A6) now has two armies on its edges. Not sourced in detail: say "assumed".
- Day 13 (strike 3, the hero, t0): Russia fires one nuclear-armed Iskander-M, a low-yield warning detonation on its own territory.

**Hero strike, the "one warhead, ring by ring" chapter.** Iskander-M, dual-capable per CRS (A11); the nuclear yield is unpublished, so 5 kt is our assumption for a low-yield warhead, not a fact (script: "the simulation assumes five kilotons, roughly a quarter of Nagasaki's 21 kt", B-row for Nagasaki from 001 and 002). Launch from a simulated site at 54.38 N, 20.64 E, target a simulated point in open forest and farmland in eastern Kaliningrad Oblast, 54.45 N, 22.15 E, on Russian soil. Engine: 98 km, bearing about 85 degrees, flight about 3.0 minutes. Airburst, 5 kt: fireball radius 0.116 km, 20 psi 0.236 km, 5 psi 1.158 km (area 4.2 square km), third-degree burns 1.217 km, 1 psi 3.134 km, little local fallout. No city file exists, so the close-up is a region view with the engine rings centred on the burst; for scale the director can show the 65 km gap and the 98 km flight line. Hook shot: the flash over the Kaliningrad oblast with the banner inside 8 s, then the 65 km gap map. Target is described only as "open ground chosen by the simulation", not as uninhabited as a fact.

**The three real option categories at the end (A18, A27 for where ladders can go):** a reply in kind or conventional reply, an alliance meeting (NATO, Article 5, A1 for nothing yet having happened), or a pause and talks. The simulation stops at the decision, as 002, 009 and 012 do. Do not pick one.

**The day after (aftermath chapter, 60 to 90 seconds, what is supported).**
- Fallout: an airburst of this size leaves little local fallout (engine). Wind assumption, SIMULATION only: a westerly breeze of about 5 m/s at the surface, typical of the south Baltic in March but not a forecast, carries the faint cloud east over Kaliningrad's own countryside toward Lithuania's border. Say "little local fallout", draw no dose map and give no number. The plume on the map is a qualitative arrow, not dose.
- What stops working, per rows only: air travel in a belt around the Baltic because the real jammer reaches about 350 km (A13); GPS; the rail line and the two roads of the gap (A7) as planners must treat them as targets; the enclave's single-track sealed rail route and ferries (A9, A22); Lithuania's decision on a ban on nuclear weapons (A5) becomes live, framed only as "the January vote would read differently"; the Estonian border (A4) is not used (date). Insurance, markets and shipping behaviour are not sourced: leave them out.
- Response: allies decide whether Article 5 is triggered by a burst on Russian soil that harmed no NATO territory (a simulation question, not answered); the three categories above.

**What the script must not claim.** No death toll for any strike (no published study for a 5 kt Baltic burst; A27's Plan A is a full war and must not be used here). No exact Iskander nuclear yield as fact. No count of Kaliningrad launchers or warheads (D). No Belarus-held warheads as fact (A15). No claim NATO plans any of this (A2). No "Russia will nuke if ..." as doctrine (A18, D). The script says "the simulation assumes" for every strike, date, wind and yield.

## D. Figures the script must NOT use

- "Lithuania's 2022 ban blocked half of what Kaliningrad imports by rail" (009 A31 wording; not supported). The governor said the ban affected 40 to 50 percent of the goods moving between Kaliningrad and the rest of Russia through Lithuania, and the rail restrictions were lifted on 2022-07-23 (B7).
- "The Suwalki gap is 40 km wide" (the Kyiv Independent text). It is about 65 km, or 40 miles; the Polish-Lithuanian border itself is about 100 km long (A6).
- "The Iskander reaches 500 km so it hits Berlin from Kaliningrad" (Berlin is about 525 km, beyond 500 km; the Bulletin uses about 350 km; the export version is about 280 km; A11).
- "Russia has 100 nuclear warheads in Kaliningrad" (a hedged Polish estimate of "up to" about 100, no evidence published; A12).
- "Kaliningrad has X Iskander launchers, Y S-400 batteries" with exact counts (2016 to 2022 reporting, out of date; A10).
- "NATO is planning to blockade Kaliningrad" (that is Russia's claim; NATO denies it; A2). And the reverse: "Russia will nuke if NATO ...". Russia's embassies and a reported diplomatic note said so; it is a threat, not an action; say "Russia has said".
- "Russia would use nuclear weapons if Kaliningrad is cut off, per doctrine" (the doctrine's condition 4 is about conventional aggression posing a "critical threat"; analysts differ on whether an isolation meets it; A18).
- "A NATO general said NATO would seize Kaliningrad" (Donahue said forces could take control in an unheard-of timeframe, in July 2025; he did not announce a plan; B9).
- "RAND proved Russia takes the Baltics in 60 hours" (60 hours is the longest time in games played in 2014 to 2015 against the 2014 posture, about the Baltic states, not the Kaliningrad cut-off; A25).
- "Zapad had 200,000 soldiers" as fact (a declared figure; independent tallies of past Zapads found far more than declared in 2017 and less than claimed in 2025; A24).
- "Kaliningrad was attacked from Lithuania in November 2026" (the claim spread online after an alert and the governor's own inspection found no threat; A3).
- "Lithuania has voted to host nuclear weapons" (only the first of three votes on repealing a constitutional ban has passed; the final vote is expected in January 2027; A5).
- "The Oreshnik in Belarus carries a nuclear warhead" (CSIS does not confirm one; "Russia says it has moved tactical nuclear weapons to Belarus; independent analysts have not confirmed it"; A15, A16).
- "Princeton Plan A says a Baltic war kills 90 million" (that is a full US-Russia strategic war; the first tactical phase is a small fraction; A27).
- Any exact Iskander nuclear yield as fact (unpublished; C).
- Anything dated after 2026-11-25 as "news": the Kaliningrad alert story was still moving when this file was compiled.
