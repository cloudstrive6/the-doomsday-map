# Iran Tests a Bomb, and Israel Answers: A Simulation

Episode 006. Draft 2, 2026-11-11. A labelled simulation on real data. It has not happened, it is not a forecast, and no date is used: the clock runs in hours and days from the test. Numbers in brackets are research and engine refs, not read aloud.

## Cold open [SIMULATION]

Four thirty-eight in the morning, central Iran: a nuclear warhead bursts on a mountain. [C]

This is a simulation, and it has not happened. [C]

Eight minutes earlier, a Jericho missile leaves a battery in central Israel. It has one target: a mountain in Iran. [A19, C]

Four days before, that mountain hosted Iran's first nuclear test. Since then an Israeli air raid has hit its tunnel entrances, and Iran has fired back. The work under the rock has not stopped. [C]

One question hangs over the map. Did the weapon reach what was buried inside? This film answers it as far as published data go. [C]

## Chapter 01: a mountain nobody can inspect [REAL DATA]

About two in the morning, Iran time, the twenty-second of June, twenty twenty-five. Seven B-2 bombers, on a round trip of thirty-six hours or more from Missouri, drop fourteen bombs of about thirteen and a half tonnes each on two enrichment sites, Fordow and Natanz. [A10, A11]

Fordow is built into a mountain about thirty kilometres north of Qom, reportedly eighty to ninety metres underground. That is the kind of target at the end of the cold open. [A9, C]

The damage is disputed. The IAEA confirmed clear and significant damage at Fordow, but says deep halls may be partly intact. Since then, no inspector has seen inside. [A13, A28]

Iran held about four hundred and forty kilograms of uranium enriched to sixty percent, below weapons grade, and that figure is more than a year stale. IAEA chief Rafael Grossi has said it could in principle fuel as many as ten bombs. That is not a claim that Iran has one. [A5, A7]

Then a new war began. In February twenty twenty-six the United States and Israel struck Iran again, and a US blockade of Iranian ports continues. What has Israel done before when it thought a neighbour was close? [A1, A2, B1]

## Chapter 02: how close, how fast [REAL HISTORY]

On the seventh of June, nineteen eighty-one, eight Israeli F-16s flew more than sixteen hundred kilometres and destroyed the Osirak reactor near Baghdad. Iran sits about sixteen hundred kilometres from Israel too. [B1, A20, A26]

On the night of the fifth of September, two thousand and seven, Israel destroyed a secret, half-built reactor in Syria. It said nothing for more than ten years, until March twenty eighteen. [B2, A26]

On the ninth of October, two thousand and six, North Korea carried out its first nuclear test. It was tiny: under one kiloton by the US intelligence chief's estimate, most other estimates between half a kiloton and two. That is the scale we use. [B5, A23]

How fast could Iran get there? One published estimate says weeks of enrichment, months to a crude device, and a year or more for a missile warhead. Suppose it gets there anyway. What happens in the first four days? [A7, C]

## Chapter 03: the test [SIMULATION]

In our simulation, Iran has left the Non-Proliferation Treaty, which the treaty allows on three months' notice. Then it fires one deep underground test, one kiloton, in a mountain in central Iran. The site is invented: it is not Fordow and it is not any real place. [A15, C]

Minute zero is the test, and every time on screen counts from it, with no calendar date. We assume the test is contained. On our map it is quiet: no fireball, no fallout, no ring numbers, because a deep test is not a surface burst. [C]

One twenty twenty-four war game, reported by the Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists, ended with Israeli air strikes failing to stop Iranian warhead work, and Israel escalating to nuclear weapons. An Israeli exercise reported in twenty twelve ended differently: Iran used a test as a bargaining chip, and Saudi Arabia moved toward its own bomb. [A24, A25]

In the twenty twenty-four game, Israel first fired a non-lethal nuclear demonstration over a remote part of Iran, then struck twenty-five targets. In the twenty twelve game, Israel weighed a defence pact with the United States while keeping a strike option. [A24, A25]

Our version is far smaller: one weapon, one site. We follow the first game, which is our choice, not a forecast. [A24, C]

So Israel has forty hours, and one problem. [C]

## Chapter 04: hour forty to day four [SIMULATION]

In our simulation, at hour forty, F-15I fighters leave Nevatim air base in southern Israel. One group flies sixteen hundred and eighty-four kilometres to the test mountain, about one hundred and twelve minutes. Another flies fifteen hundred and eighty-six kilometres to the Isfahan tunnels, about one hundred and six. [C]

They carry the bunker busters Israel has. It does not own the bomb of about thirteen and a half tonnes America used on Fordow, or the B-2 that carries it. At Isfahan in twenty twenty-five the storage tunnels were too deep for even that bomb, so the strikes went for the entrances. Israel's aim here is the same: the entrances, not the halls below. [A10, A13, C]

Hour forty-two. As the raids land, Iran fires. One missile in the engine stands for a salvo from western Iran, twelve hundred kilometres to Nevatim, six and a half minutes away. In June twenty twenty-five Iran fired about five hundred and fifty missiles, and by Israel's count about nine in ten were intercepted. [A21, C]

More than two days pass. In our simulation, the entrance raids do not end the work, as in the twenty twenty-four game. The digging goes on, and so does the warhead. [A24, C]

Day four. In our scenario, Israel's cabinet decides that only a nuclear weapon addresses a buried target. One Jericho Two missile, a reported range of fifteen hundred kilometres or more, flies sixteen hundred and seventy kilometres in eight minutes. Israel has never confirmed it has nuclear weapons; the ten-kiloton yield is entirely our assumption. [A18, A19, C]

The launch comes ninety-six hours after the test, and the flight takes eight minutes. That is the clock from the cold open. [C]

No other nuclear weapon is used. Two nuclear events, eleven kilotons together. [C]

What does ten kilotons do to a mountain? [C]

## Chapter 05: one warhead, ring by ring [SIMULATION]

In our simulation, the warhead bursts on the ground at the mountain. We choose a ground burst; the engine offers only air and ground bursts. Every number here is the engine's. [C]

A burst on the ground lifts earth into the fireball, and that earth comes back down as fallout. The fireball is one hundred and ninety metres in radius, about three hundred and eighty metres across. [C]

Twenty pounds per square inch of overpressure reaches two hundred and forty metres. [C]

Five psi reaches one point one seven kilometres, an area of about four point three square kilometres. On flat ground that flattens most buildings. This is a mountain, and rock changes the result: we give no number for the tunnels. [C]

Third-degree burns reach one point six kilometres. Broken windows, three point two. We put the site in thinly populated country, a choice, so no town sits inside these rings. [C]

In our simulation the wind blows from the west, our assumption and not a forecast, so the plume runs east and north-east over desert, away from Qom, Kashan and Tehran. We give no dose and no distance, because the engine has none. [C]

Did it destroy the deep halls? We do not know. Depth estimates for real sites differ: one estimate for Pickaxe Mountain near Natanz says eighty to a hundred metres, another repeats up to six hundred. Penetration estimates differ too, and no published model covers this site. [A9, A10, A12, C]

## Chapter 06: the day after [SIMULATION]

In our simulation, the question from the opening stays open. The first change on the map is the dark around the mountain: since June twenty twenty-five, no inspector has seen inside Fordow, Natanz or Isfahan, and the stockpile figure is more than a year stale. [A5, A28, C]

Did the weapon reach the halls? The second change is a plume, drifting east over the Dasht-e Kavir desert and thinning away from the cities. Fallout travels downwind, and the engine gives no dose. [C]

The IAEA has said on the real sites is that deep halls may be partly intact. Nothing in our simulation updates it. [A13, C]

Still unanswered, the third change is the missile tracks. Iran fires at Israel again. In June twenty twenty-five, by Israel's count, about nine in ten of its missiles were intercepted, and at least thirty-one hit populated areas or key sites. [A21, C]

What is buried, and what is left? The fourth change is the water. The US blockade of Iranian ports and a contested Strait of Hormuz are real pressure points. What a nuclear strike would do to shipping, no published study says. [A1, A2, C]

The question follows the treaty map too. Israel never joined the Non-Proliferation Treaty. In real life Iran's parliament has had exit bills; in our simulation it has left. In the twenty twelve game, a neighbour moved toward its own bomb. [A15, A25, C]

And the question needs someone to ask it. In our simulation one channel stays open: Qatar, the real go-between today. [A2, C]

The mountain is still there, and so is the question. [C]
