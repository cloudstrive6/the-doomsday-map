## C. Simulation assumptions (SIMULATION tag, stated as assumptions in the script)

Engine file: `scenario.json` and `scenario.computed.json` (all blast numbers below are from the engine; model: Glasstone and Dolan scaling calibrated to NUKEMAP Hiroshima figures, A28). Every choice here is the simulation's, not a fact.

**Escalation logic.** There is none, on purpose. This episode is a scale study, not a war: one weapon, one point, "a simulated attacker" never named (no country is accused). The question it answers is what the 1961 test (A1, A3) would look like over a city. The 50 Mt bomb was never deployed and could not ride a missile (A12, A30), so the simulation says plainly that delivery is simulated: a single heavy bomber release 929 km east of the target over the North Atlantic (location simulated), engine flight time 62 min. t0 is that release; the clock the script uses is the burst, T plus 0:00. No treaty, alert or crisis is claimed beyond the real why-now rows (A20, A23, A27, B10); no date is set (t0 2026-11-24 15:30 UTC is only for the globe lighting: late morning in New York, daylight on the east coast).

**Strike groups.**
1. `hero-50mt`: one 50 Mt airburst, ground zero 40.7075 N, 74.0113 W (lower Manhattan, public landmark point). 50 Mt is the Soviet figure for the real test (A1); published range 50 to 58 (A2). ## D. Figures the script must NOT use

- "Tsar Bomba was 100 megatons" (design yield only; the test was about 50, A1, A3).
- "Tsar Bomba was exactly 50 megatons" without hedging (US estimates were 57; range 50 to 58, A2).
- "The shock wave circled the Earth three times in X hours" and any "36 hours and 27 minutes" figure (one popular site; not in the sources opened). Say only "was recorded circling the Earth three times" (A10).
- "The mushroom cloud was 40 miles tall" as precise (sources give 64 to 67 km, about 40 miles, so it is only a rough match; use km, A7).
- "Windows broke 1,000 km away" (the flash was visible at 1,000 km; windows were partly broken to about 900 km, A8, A9).
- "Tsar Bomba was dropped on the island with no fallout" or "clean" without saying "about 97 percent less fallout than the full design" (A3).
- "The Tsar Bomba is in service" or "Russia has Tsar Bombas" (never deployed, A12).
- "Russia tested a nuclear weapon in October or November 2025" (it tested the Burevestnik and Poseidon, not an explosive nuclear test, A23, A25; the Nature piece's "two nuclear weapons" wording is the Kremlin's claim about missile tests).
- "The US resumed nuclear explosive tests" (Wright said non-nuclear, A24).
- "Russia has 6,000 warheads" or "5,459 warheads" (the 5,459 and 1,718 figures are from a non-authoritative site, globalmilitary.net; FAS and SIPRI give about 5,420 total, 1,796 deployed strategic, A17).
- "The US and Russia are still limited by New START" (it expired on 2026-02-05, A20).
- "The W88 is 800 kilotons" or "a modern warhead is 800 kilotons" without naming the Russian range (A21, A22).
