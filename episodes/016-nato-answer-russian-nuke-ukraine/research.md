**Framing check, read first.** As of 2026-11-23 Russia has NOT used, tested or detonated a nuclear weapon in or near Ukraine, and no real NATO nuclear or conventional response plan has been announced (A1). What HAS happened in the last 60 days, and may be used as "why this question is live": on 2026-09-29 and 2026-09-30 Russian embassies (London, Dublin) said any British or NATO action to isolate or seize Kaliningrad would be met with "all means", nuclear weapons included, and Reuters reported the same threat in a formal note to NATO (A3); NATO Secretary-General Mark Rutte answered "We are a defensive alliance. No new formal Russian doctrine has been published in 2026; the operative text is still the 2024 revision (A5). Note for the script: the Kaliningrad claim is Russia's allegation, not a NATO plan, and it is the subject of episode 017, so do not blur the two. The episode is a hypothetical and must say so; it must not name a real date for the simulated strike. Real dates to avoid or acknowledge: the Abu Dhabi talks are live and a ceasefire headline could land any day; Lithuania's final nuclear-ban vote is expected in January 2027; the fifth anniversary of the invasion is 2027-02-24; NATO's Steadfast Noon exercise normally runs mid-October (2024-10-14, 2025-10-13 to 2025-10-24), so it will not collide. ## C. Simulation assumptions (SIMULATION tag, each one stated as an assumption in the script)

Decided 2026-11-23 by the scenario designer. Effect numbers come only from `scenario.computed.json` (`python -m studio scenario`). Everything here is a simulation choice, not a prediction and not a fact. No real NATO or US plan exists (D5, A27), so every NATO move below is labelled "we assume". Rows relied on only as labelled assumptions: A12 yield and range (single-source), A11, A21, A29. Not used at all: A4 (unopened source), A7.

**Escalation logic and why (all simulation choices).**

  - Hour 36 to 72: the decision the episode is built on. Options on the map: (1) a nuclear response, shown as an option NATO openly declines (Edmonds: "politically infeasible" on a non-nuclear state's soil, A24); (2) a conventional strike on the launch platform, the Iskander unit or the Black Sea Fleet (Vershbow, A23; Edmonds, A24), which would cross a line Russia has called escalation (A3, A30); (3) stay the course: more arms, sanctions, isolation; (4) press Ukraine to settle, judged unwise (A24). - Which line is crossed: a conventional NATO strike on Russian forces is the first step that would make NATO a direct combatant; this is the point Edmonds and the Atlantic Council experts name (A23, A24). The script says "the analysts' red line", not NATO's.
