Compiled 2026-11-05 (NZDT). Every claim the script uses is listed with its sources. Two reputable, independent sources per fact is the rule; single-source items are marked SINGLE-SOURCE and must be softened in the script ("about", "one estimate puts it at") or cut. **Framing check, read first.** As of 2026-11-05 there has been NO real blockade or quarantine of Taiwan, and no named large-scale PLA exercise around Taiwan in 2026 (A1). The episode can honestly open on "this is already starting" with A2, as long as the blockade itself is labelled SIMULATION. Upcoming real dates the script may collide with: APEC summit in Shenzhen 2026-11-18 to 2026-11-19, Taiwan local elections 2026-11-28, and the PLA's usual November to December drill season (A5, A7, A1). ## C. Simulation assumptions (SIMULATION tag, stated as assumptions in the script)

Decided 2026-11-05 by the scenario designer, from the research desk's suggestions. Numbers come from `scenario.computed.json` (studio/scenario.py).

- No calendar date is given on screen: the crisis runs on day numbers (Day 1 to Day 23) so it cannot read as a prediction or collide with APEC (2026-11-18 to 19) or Taiwan's local elections (2026-11-28). The engine's clock is set to a December night for the sun position only.
- Phase 1, quarantine (CSIS model, A35): on Day 1 Beijing announces customs inspection of every ship bound for Taiwan; more than three dozen coast guard ships take station off the ports; the navy stays over the horizon. It opens from the real October 2026 patrols (A2).
- Shipping behaviour on Days 2 to 4 (insurers and owners hold tankers back; Taiwan cuts gas to heavy industry first) is the simulation's, not sourced.
- Phase 2, blockade: on Day 6 a Taiwanese escort attempt is blocked without shots, and Beijing declares closure zones around Kaohsiung, Keelung, Taichung and the three LNG terminals, modelled on Justice Mission 2025's zones (B7, A12).
- Day 11: LNG stocks reach zero, matching the legal 11-day reserve (A15) and CSIS's finding that gas ran out within about 10 days (A16).
- Day 14: a US and Japanese escorted convoy from the south through the Bashi Channel (A10). Day 16: a PLA destroyer fires on an escort; China strikes Kadena with a DF-17 from Fujian (engine: 1,087 km, about 7 minutes) and Andersen AFB, Guam with a conventional DF-26 (3,426 km, about 13 minutes), as in the CNAS game (A37). Launch sites are simulated.
- Days 19 to 22: US submarines thin the blockade fleet (direction of CSIS's invasion game losses, A33); the blockade is failing. Beijing's calculation follows A29 and A36 (first use considered when defeat threatens the regime).
- Day 23, 04:10 Taipei: one DF-26 with a nuclear warhead (A30) from a simulated site in southern China (25.0 N, 114.5 E) at a US carrier strike group in the Philippine Sea at 23.8 N, 128.0 E: about 650 km east of Hualien, 285 km south-east of Kadena. Engine: 1,373 km, flight about 7 minutes.
- Yield 10 kilotons, airburst a few hundred metres up: the top of the Pentagon's "below 10 kilotons" estimate for China's low-yield warheads (A29), about half of Nagasaki's 21 kt (B8). Engine: fireball radius 0.15 km (about 300 m across), 20 psi 0.30 km, 5 psi 1.46 km, third-degree burns 1.62 km, 1 psi 3.95 km, little local fallout. The script describes rings in land terms ("would flatten most buildings on land") because there is no open source on blast damage to a modern carrier; it does not give a death toll.
- The simulation stops at the US president's decision. The three options shown are real categories: a low-yield W76-2 reply (A31), a conventional reply, or diplomacy (A36). The script does not pick one.
- No SLCM-N (A31), no casualty figures for the carrier group, no named ships or captains.

## D. Figures the script must NOT use

- "China is blockading Taiwan" or "a blockade began in 2026" (false as of 2026-11-05; only coast guard patrols and drills, A1, A2).
- "Taiwan has only 8 days of LNG" (outdated 2022-era legal minimum; use 11 to 12 days, A15).
- "TSMC or Taiwan makes 90 percent of the world's chips" (wrong; the 60 to 90 percent range applies only to the most advanced chips, A18.).
- "Half of all world trade goes through the Taiwan Strait" (the 48 percent is a count of container ships in seven months of 2022; by value it is about a fifth of maritime trade, A19, A20).
- "A blockade would cost the world 5 trillion dollars" (no Bloomberg dollar figure exists for the blockade; say "about 5 percent of world GDP", A21).
- "China has 1,500 nuclear warheads" (that was a 2022 Pentagon projection for 2035, dropped from later reports; current figure about 600 to 620, A28).
- "China has 600 deployed nuclear warheads" (most are stored; only about 34 deployed per SIPRI, A28).
- "The DF-17 is a nuclear missile" (FAS says dual-capable claims are unsubstantiated, A24).
- "China fired missiles over Taiwan in December 2025" (in Justice Mission 2025 rockets landed near Taiwan but missiles were not fired over the island; the overflights were August 2022, B4, B7).
- "The Taiwan Strait is 100 miles wide at its narrowest" as a precise figure (Britannica says 160 km; most sources say 126 to 130 km; use the range in A8).
- "Kadena is 519 km from Taipei" (an outlier on one distance site; the figure is about 640 to 670 km, A10).
- "CSIS predicts the US would lose two carriers" (the 2026 invasion year in CSIS's game was a fictional setting, and two carriers is the base case of a wargame, not a forecast; say "in the wargame", A33).
- "Japan's 7th Fleet" or "the US has several carriers in Japan" (one forward-deployed carrier, USS George Washington, A26).
- The US SLCM-N nuclear cruise missile as an option in the war (not deployed before 2032 at the earliest, A31).
- Any exact Eisenhower "bullet" wording beyond the short phrase in B1 (only a paraphrase in the State Department editorial note is confirmed).
