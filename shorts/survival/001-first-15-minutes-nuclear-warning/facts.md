# 001 First 15 Minutes After a Nuclear Warning: fact sheet

Compiled 2026-11-17 by the research desk for the Survival Clock pilot (format:
`Z:\Bryce-Brain\wiki\answers\survival-clock-reel-breakdown.md`; backlog: `../backlog.md`). Every quote below was read
on the page or document named, on 2026-11-17. Dashes inside quotes are normalised to hyphens. "Script wording" is the
rounded line the script may use. Status: OK = two or more independent official sources agree; SINGLE-SOURCE = one
source (soften with "about" or "one study" or cut); MODEL = a modelled scenario, must be labelled as such on screen.

**Framing check, read first.**
- 2026-11-01 and 2026-11-02: hacked alert apps in Ukraine and Russia showed FALSE nuclear and chemical alerts; both
  governments said there was no radiation threat (https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/2/nuclear-radiation-in-russia-ukraine-no-just-hacked-warning-alerts).
  The "stay tuned" beat should say "official radio, TV or alerts", and the phone mock-up must be generic, not a copy of
  any real app or OS, and labelled ILLUSTRATIVE.
- The video gives advice for a nuclear attack warning or detonation. It does not depict a real place being hit. If a
  map is used in the hook, label it SIMULATION or SCHEMATIC, per the channel rules.
- No real event in the last 60 days changes the US, UK or EU guidance used here. The newest official document is the
  EU population preparedness guidance of 2026-10-30 (not needed for this pilot).

## C. Figures that differ between sources, and which one to use

1. **Time before fallout:** Ready.gov "10 minutes or more"; FEMA PrepTalks "at least 10 - 15 minutes" and, in the
   same guide, "15 minutes or more"; LLNL "at least several minutes" (its Los Angeles model has fallout arriving
   "~15 minutes after detonation"). **Use "10 minutes or more" (Ready.gov).** It is the current public page and the
   most cautious. The clock can show a "10:00 TO FALLOUT" pill.
2. **How long to stay inside:** Ready.gov, CDC and the FEMA-72 template say "at least 24 hours"; FEMA-72 Tactic 1
   also says "up to 24 hours or until informed it is safe"; FEMA PrepTalks says "12-24 hours"; UK says "one to two
   days"; LLNL's minimum is "at least the first hour". **Use "at least 24 hours, unless officials tell you
   otherwise".**
3. **Missile warning time:** FEMA-72 "15-30 minutes" (to state and local agencies); HI-EMA "12 to 15 minutes" (for
   Hawaii); FEMA PrepTalks "possibly 15-20 minutes or more". **Use "only minutes" in speech; if a number is shown,
   "AS LITTLE AS 15 TO 30 MIN (FEMA PLANNING FIGURE)".** Do not claim a viewer will get 15 minutes: the format clock
   is a device, so label it "FIRST 15 MINUTES", never "YOU HAVE 15 MINUTES".
4. **Protection factor of a basement:** LLNL Table 1 "10-20"; LLNL text calls PF 10 the minimum adequate shelter;
   other LLNL figures put underground areas of large buildings above 100. **Use "about 10 times less" for a house
   basement and "about 100 times less" for the core of a large building.** Do not use "PF 40" (often quoted online,
   not found in a source read for this file).
5. **Iodine pill timing (only if A35 is used):** CDC "within 24 hours before or 4 hours after exposure"; UK "between
   24 hours before and 2 hours after", "still be useful up to 8 hours after". **Do not give timing in the pilot;**
   just say "only when officials tell you".
6. **Decay example:** REMM's hypothetical gives 10% at 8 hours; the 7-10 rule gives 10% at 7 hours. **Say "about a
   tenth after seven hours"** and keep REMM's numbers off screen unless both are shown as "about".

## D. What NOT to say (myths the sources contradict)

1. **"Get in the car and drive away from the blast."** CDC: "Cars do not provide good protection from radioactive
   material."; LLNL: fallout winds can exceed 50 mph and evacuation "will likely result in traffic jams".
2. **"Take iodine tablets or iodised salt straight away to protect yourself from radiation."** CDC: KI "protects
   only the thyroid", only on officials' instruction, and "Do not use table salt ... as a substitute".
3. **"If your windows break or the room is not sealed airtight, the shelter is useless, so leave."** LLNL:
   "Buildings do not have to be air-tight. Broken windows do not greatly reduce the protection offered by a shelter."

Also keep out: "you have exactly 15 minutes" (see C1, C3); "you need a gas mask first" (FEMA PrepTalks: do not spend
time on respiratory protection, go inside); "duck and cover saves you from everything" (LLNL notes it helps only
against flying glass and the heat pulse and "requires reacting properly to the bright flash within the first few
seconds"); "stay in the shelter for two weeks" (no source for the general public; HI-EMA's 14 days is a supplies
figure, not a shelter time); any death toll for a named real city that is not the labelled MODEL in A31.
